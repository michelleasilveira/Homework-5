Findings

Composition: Most students are from GP, with a modest female majority; MS contributes a smaller, more balanced cohort. Urban (U) addresses dominate. These imbalances should be considered when comparing groups.

Distributions: Final grades (G3.math, G3.por) cluster between ~10–15 with mild right tails. Absences show a low median but long right tail (extreme outliers), so medians/IQRs are more robust than means/SDs.

Group comparisons: Differences in grade centers by sex are small relative to within-group variability. Splitting further by school or address doesn’t reveal strong or consistent shifts in grade centers, though small cells increase uncertainty.

Strongest correlations are between each final grade and prior period grades (e.g., G3 with G2/G1), matching academic expectations.

Absences have a weak to modest negative relationship with G3 (more absences ↔︎ slightly lower final grade). This pattern is visually consistent across schools, sexes, and addresses; we see little evidence of interaction by these factors.

In this joined sample, prior achievement is the primary predictor of final grades, while demographics (sex, address) show minor effects. Absences matter, but modestly, and their impact appears similar across subgroups. Any inference should account for sample imbalance (GP ≫ MS) and outliers in absences.
